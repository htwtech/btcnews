UBS CEO Sergio Ermotti confirmed the bank is building digital asset infrastructure and exploring targeted crypto offerings for wealth management clients.

"These offerings are intended to complement our existing services, not replace them," he said. The bank is in partner selection mode, with expansion planned as internal readiness aligns with client demand.

UBS is approaching this as measured entry. Ermotti was clear at Davos: blockchain convergence with traditional banking is coming, and for the financial services sector, margin pressure is real.

Private banking clients already access crypto through exchanges. What they want is their wealth manager to custody it alongside bonds and equities.

UBS is planning expansion gradually. Slow enough to test partners and risk, fast enough to move before clients leave. The bank's blockchain track record is there - a tokenized gold pilot in early 2025, a successful Digital Cash pilot in November 2024 for cross-border payments. Julius Baer, a smaller Swiss peer, has been offering crypto products since 2020 through Amina Bank.

When integration becomes an operational checkbox instead of a strategic bet, institutional adoption stops being news.
