# Bullish on Bitcoin: recap

Bullish on Bitcoin aired today. [Dan Held](https://x.com/danheld) sat down with [Alexei Zamyatin](https://x.com/alexeiZamyatin), and the conversation went further than most Bitcoin podcasts do.

On the market, Dan's read is straightforward: Bitcoin at $80K only looks weak if you're benchmarking against a peak price that existed for maybe a week in 2021. Look at averages, look at who's left holding, and the asset is in a stronger position than sentiment suggests. Bear sentiment has washed out, weak hands are leaving, and the next catalyst (real follow-through on the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, geopolitical shift, anything really) could flip things in minutes. He referenced 2013, where Bitcoin chopped sideways for seven months before running from $100 to $1,200.

ETF outflows during this correction were minimal. Institutional holders held, and that's a harder data point to dismiss than most cycle commentary right now.

Quantum computing got a serious treatment. Dan's position: the threat is real long-term, but the panic scenario (state actor dumps Satoshi's coins, Bitcoin collapses) is bad game theory. Any entity sophisticated enough to crack a private key would sign a public message first, work through OTC desks, sell in structured tranches over years. The real concern is what surrounds the debate: journalists calling it "Bitcoin is broken" regardless of what technically happened, and the political fight over what to do with coins that never moved. Burn them, leave them, recycle them into the issuance schedule. Each option carries its own reputational problem.

BTCFi was where the conversation got most concrete. Alexei put the gap plainly: cross-chain BTC swap volume is around $3–4M a month, against hundreds of billions in centralised exchange BTC liquidity. Bitcoin still has no user-friendly self-custody tooling, no good multisig UX, and most of what's been built targets power users, not retail. Dan's read on why: there hasn't been a retail-led bull run since BTCFi infrastructure matured enough to absorb one, with ETH DeFi Summer being the closest reference point for what that pull looks like. The reputational issue with some protocols (illicit fund routing, Thor Chain came up by name) is real friction — intent-based systems with institutional market makers are a better fit structurally, because those counterparties won't trade with bad actors by default.
