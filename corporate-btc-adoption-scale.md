# Corporate BTC Adoption is Broadening. The Ability to Scale Still Isn’t.

What stands out in the latest corporate Bitcoin data is not just the growth itself, but how much wider the footprint has actually become. 

The footprint is widening across both companies and geographies. More treasury programs are appearing outside the obvious names, and more corporate BTC is now being held outside the U.S. than earlier in the cycle.

![Corporate BTC Footprint](https://x.com/btc_board/article/2044523235471937888/media/2044502881882005504)

## A Top-Heavy Treasury Structure

Total corporate BTC keeps climbing, but the structure underneath the hood is still heavily top-weighted. That part looks undeniably real. 

The initial data shows a structure that is dominated by the heavyweights. A tiny fraction of treasury programs continue to account for almost all the meaningful scale, which is exactly why the headline numbers often overstate how broad the demand base really is. 

But the second chart matters because it entirely removes the easy objection. Once Strategy is stripped out, the growth story does not disappear. The other cohort has expanded meaningfully, which tells us this is no longer just a one-company story.

![Excluding MSTR Cohort](https://x.com/btc_board/article/2044523235471937888/media/2044503347479359488)

## Broader Participation ≠ Broader Buying Power

Even excluding the biggest whale, the long tail has grown meaningfully. The data pushes the point further—corporate Bitcoin holdings outside the U.S. have grown across a wider set of countries. This suggests that the institutional adoption narrative is bleeding into more markets, not just more firms.

That does not mean scale has decentralized.

![Geographic Distribution](https://x.com/btc_board/article/2044523235471937888/media/2044505361407713280)

It hasn’t. The long tail is getting larger, and the surface area is widening. But the actual ability to build a meaningful position still appears concentrated in a relatively small group of issuers equipped with the best access to capital and the strongest market support. 

We think that distinction matters significantly more than the raw adoption narrative the market likes to sell. Broader participation and broader buying power are not the same thing. The latest data tells a clear story: corporate Bitcoin is vastly more geographically dispersed today, while the real balance sheet firepower remains aggressively clustered near the top. 

That is probably the most honest read here. The corporate Bitcoin story is absolutely broadening, but the structure underneath it is still far less decentralized than the headlines imply.
