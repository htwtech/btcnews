# Wrapped Bitcoin: Bringing BTC Liquidity Into Ethereum DeFi

Wrapped Bitcoin is one of the simplest and most important ideas in BTCFi: take Bitcoin liquidity and make it usable inside Ethereum’s smart contract economy.

WBTC is an ERC-20 token backed 1:1 by Bitcoin. Each WBTC represents BTC held in reserve by a custodian, allowing Bitcoin holders to access Ethereum-based DeFi without selling their BTC exposure.

That design made WBTC one of the core bridges between Bitcoin capital and Ethereum DeFi.

Bitcoin itself is highly secure, liquid, and widely recognized, but it was not designed to interact directly with decentralized applications, lending protocols, automated market makers, or smart contract-based financial products.

WBTC solves that compatibility problem by representing BTC in a format Ethereum applications can understand.

It is not the same as native BTC.

That distinction matters.

WBTC expands Bitcoin’s utility, but it also introduces new trust assumptions around custody, issuance, redemption, governance, and Ethereum execution.

## What WBTC does

WBTC allows Bitcoin liquidity to move into Ethereum DeFi.

Once BTC is converted into WBTC, users can interact with applications like DEXs, lending markets, collateralized debt systems, and structured yield products. This gives BTC holders access to financial activity that is difficult or impossible to perform directly on Bitcoin L1.

The main use cases are straightforward: trading, lending, borrowing, liquidity provision, collateral usage, and yield strategies.

For Ethereum DeFi, WBTC also matters because it brings one of crypto’s deepest and most liquid assets into smart contract markets. BTC becomes usable as collateral and liquidity, while Ethereum applications gain access to a larger capital base.

That is the core WBTC value proposition: Bitcoin liquidity, Ethereum composability.

## How WBTC works

WBTC is issued when Bitcoin is deposited with a custodian. The corresponding amount of WBTC is minted on Ethereum, maintaining a 1:1 backing model.

When WBTC is redeemed, the token is burned and the underlying BTC is released.

This mint-and-burn process is designed to keep WBTC supply aligned with Bitcoin reserves. Issuance and burning are visible on-chain, which gives users and applications more transparency than many opaque wrapped-asset systems.

BitGo acts as the custodian. Kyber Network and Ren were among the founding participants, helping launch the project and support early liquidity.

WBTC was first announced on October 26, 2018, and officially launched on January 31, 2019.

## Why WBTC became important

Before WBTC, Bitcoin liquidity was mostly isolated from Ethereum DeFi.

BTC holders could own the strongest monetary asset in crypto, but they could not easily use it inside Ethereum lending, trading, or yield markets. At the same time, Ethereum DeFi protocols wanted access to Bitcoin liquidity, but needed a standardized token format.

WBTC solved that by turning BTC into an ERC-20 asset.

That made integrations much easier for wallets, exchanges, DEXs, lending protocols, and DeFi applications. Instead of supporting Bitcoin-specific infrastructure, Ethereum-based apps could integrate WBTC like any other ERC-20 token.

This is why WBTC became widely used across protocols such as Uniswap, Curve, Compound, MakerDAO, and other DeFi platforms.

It gave Bitcoin holders a way to access DeFi, and it gave Ethereum DeFi a way to absorb Bitcoin liquidity.

## BTC and WBTC are not the same asset experience

Native Bitcoin and Wrapped Bitcoin serve different roles.

BTC operates on the Bitcoin blockchain, where the priority is security, decentralization, and monetary settlement.

WBTC exists on Ethereum, where the priority is smart contract compatibility, composability, and access to DeFi applications.

That difference creates very different user experiences.

BTC is mainly used as a store of value, settlement asset, and payment asset. WBTC is used as a DeFi asset: collateral, liquidity, trading pair, and yield-bearing input across Ethereum-based applications.

WBTC transactions can also benefit from Ethereum’s faster block times compared with Bitcoin’s roughly 10-minute average block interval, although Ethereum network fees and congestion can still affect the user experience.

The tradeoff is trust.

Native BTC does not depend on a custodian to exist. WBTC does. Users must accept that the underlying BTC is held by a custodian and that the wrapped token depends on the integrity of the minting, burning, and reserve process.

## Strengths of WBTC

WBTC’s biggest strength is liquidity access.

It lets Bitcoin holders use BTC value across Ethereum DeFi without selling into another asset. This expands Bitcoin’s utility and gives DeFi protocols access to BTC-denominated liquidity.

The second strength is compatibility.

Because WBTC is an ERC-20 token, it can be integrated into Ethereum applications with much less friction than native BTC. That made it easier for DEXs, lending markets, wallets, and structured products to support Bitcoin exposure.

The third strength is transparency.

WBTC’s issuance and burning can be observed on-chain, and the 1:1 reserve model gives users a clearer framework for understanding supply and backing.

WBTC also helped normalize BTC as productive collateral inside DeFi. It became one of the earliest examples of Bitcoin being used beyond passive holding while still preserving BTC price exposure.

## Weaknesses and risks

WBTC’s main weakness is custody.

The model depends on Bitcoin being held by a custodian. That introduces a trust assumption that does not exist when holding native BTC directly.

For users who care about full self-custody and trust minimization, this is the biggest tradeoff.

The second risk is network dependency.

WBTC lives on Ethereum, so users inherit Ethereum-specific risks: smart contract exposure, network congestion, gas fees, and Ethereum ecosystem execution risk.

The third risk is wrapped-asset complexity.

WBTC may feel simple from the user side, but the system depends on reserve management, minting, burning, merchant flows, custody processes, and governance. These layers need to work correctly for the token to maintain trust.

That is why WBTC should not be treated as identical to BTC.

It gives BTC more utility, but it also changes the risk profile.

## WBTC vs BTC

Bitcoin is the native asset of the Bitcoin network. Its main strengths are security, decentralization, liquidity, and monetary credibility.

Wrapped Bitcoin is a representation of BTC on Ethereum. Its main strength is usability inside smart contract applications.

BTC is closer to base money.

WBTC is closer to a DeFi-compatible Bitcoin receipt.

That does not make WBTC bad. It makes it different.

For users who want maximum trust minimization, native BTC remains the cleaner asset. For users who want to borrow, lend, trade, or deploy BTC value across Ethereum DeFi, WBTC provides a practical route.

The choice depends on what the user wants to do.

Hold Bitcoin with fewer external assumptions: native BTC.

Use Bitcoin liquidity inside Ethereum DeFi: WBTC.

## The role of WBTC in BTCFi

WBTC became one of the foundational assets of BTCFi before BTCFi had a clean category name.

It showed that Bitcoin liquidity could be used inside DeFi markets at scale. It helped unlock BTC-backed lending, BTC liquidity pools, collateralized borrowing, and yield strategies across Ethereum.

At the same time, WBTC also revealed the core tension in Bitcoin finance.

Users want BTC utility, but they do not always want wrapped assets, custodians, or external trust assumptions.

That is why newer Bitcoin-native systems often position themselves against wrapped BTC models. They are trying to give BTC financial utility without relying on the same custody and bridging assumptions.

Still, WBTC remains important because it solved the first major version of the problem: making Bitcoin usable in DeFi.

It is not the final form of Bitcoin finance.

But it is one of the assets that proved there was real demand for BTC beyond passive holding.
