# How BTC Leaves Mainnet and Starts Working

Bitcoin on mainnet is pristine. It is also, for most users, idle. The moment BTC leaves L1, it stops being just something you hold. It starts doing work. It becomes collateral in DeFi, moves into yield strategies, or turns into the asset you need if you want access to Stacks, Rootstock, ICP, or Merlin. 

That is what this market really is: not a catalog of wrappers for its own sake, but a set of routes for turning BTC into something usable. One clarification up front: native BTC exists only on Bitcoin. Everywhere else, users are holding some BTC-backed representation with its own minting flow, trust model, and redemption rules.

## If the goal is liquidity

This is still the easiest part of the market to read. Some BTC assets win because they are the fastest route into the deepest parts of DeFi. WBTC remains the obvious starting point. It became the default DeFi version of BTC because integrations came first and liquidity followed. This is a custodial structure, but if the job is getting bitcoin into lending markets, DEX pools, and collateral venues with the least friction, that tradeoff is exactly what a lot of users accept.

cbBTC is the cleaner exchange-native version of the same logic. Coinbase’s own wrapped BTC flow allows users to withdraw BTC to supported networks and receive cbBTC there. It currently targets Base, Ethereum, Solana, and Arbitrum. However, compliance layers mean cbBTC is unavailable in some jurisdictions, including Canada, Georgia, and Japan. It matters because if a user is already on Coinbase, it is one of the shortest ways to land in on-chain BTC liquidity.

FBTC sits nearby, though the angle is slightly different. It is pushed as an omnichain BTC asset rather than something designed for one venue. If you expect your BTC to move across ecosystems rather than stay parked in one pool, this is where the route usually leads.

## If the goal is yield

A growing number of users are no longer looking for BTC that is merely usable. They want BTC that stays liquid while also earning. LBTC is one of the cleaner direct routes into that trade. You send BTC to the generated deposit address, wait for six Bitcoin confirmations, and LBTC mints to the destination wallet. It is available on 15+ chains and is inherently yield-bearing, though users should note that unstaking takes 9 days. This directness is a big part of the appeal — you start with native BTC without needing another wrapper first.

SolvBTC follows a similar logic as an on-chain Bitcoin reserve asset. It is fully backed by Bitcoin under defined rules for minting, custody, and redemption. Redemptions happen on a set schedule, and that schedule depends on the specific product. This is a critical operational detail that often gets overlooked until a user tries to exit the position.

eBTC from ether.fi is a different kind of yield route. It is not really the cleanest first step from native BTC; it is what users reach for once they already hold wrapped BTC and want more out of it. eBTC is backed by LBTC, allows deposits of LBTC and WBTC, and focuses on dual yield through staking plus restaking with multiple points programs layered on top. 

## If the trust model matters more than convenience

Some users are perfectly fine with exchange-backed or custodian-backed BTC if the liquidity is there. Others are not. Threshold’s tBTC is built for users who want BTC in programmable environments without taking the most obvious custodial shortcut. It requires BTC, a Bitcoin wallet, an Ethereum wallet, and ETH for gas, making it a more complex but less custodial route.

sBTC makes more sense when prioritizing the destination over the wrapper type. This is the primary rail for the Stacks ecosystem, with a minimum minting threshold of 0.001 BTC. Similarly, ckBTC on the Internet Computer focuses on making BTC cheap and fast to use inside ICP, with a tiny transfer fee of 0.0000001 ckBTC and 1:1 backing.

rBTC on Rootstock is one of the older BTC-to-smart-contract routes still standing. Its PowPeg guide requires a 0.005 BTC minimum and explicitly warns that sending less than that can lead to loss of funds. These routes are smaller than the liquidity-first segment, but the users choosing them usually have very specific ideological or technical motivations.

## If what you really want is one ecosystem

Some BTC assets only need to be judged against the ecosystem they are meant to enter. Merlin BTC is the prime example for Merlin Chain. The bridge supports bidirectional movement between L1 and the L2 environment, with estimated costs around 0.003 BTC to enter and 0.00002 BTC to exit. You don't choose Merlin BTC because it's universal; you choose it because you want Merlin itself.

UBTC on Hyperliquid follows a similar decision logic. Users can deposit BTC directly into Hyperliquid via the Unit Protocol. The minimum deposit is 0.0003 BTC, and it typically takes 30 minutes to arrive. In the app, it appears as BTC, but on the HyperCore mainnet, it operates as UBTC.

## Navigating the choice

For pure DeFi liquidity, WBTC and cbBTC are still the defaults. For yield, LBTC and SolvBTC are cleaner routes from native BTC, while eBTC is better suited to those already deep in the wrapped stack. If the goal is access to one specific ecosystem, the answer is to use the native asset built for that rail instead of forcing a general-purpose wrapper.

The branding makes this market look crowded, but in practice, most users are just deciding four things: where BTC is easiest to use, where it can earn, how much trust they are willing to outsource, and which ecosystem they actually want to enter.
