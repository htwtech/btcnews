# Avalon Labs Is Building the BTC Lending Layer

Avalon Labs sits in one of the most practical parts of BTCFi: lending.

A lot of Bitcoin finance narratives start with the same broad idea: BTC should become more productive. That is true, but users do not interact with broad ideas. They interact with rates, collateral rules, liquidation risk, stablecoin liquidity, withdrawal paths, and whether the product feels reliable under market stress.

Avalon is focused on that layer.

The platform combines DeFi and CeDeFi lending infrastructure for BTC and BTC liquid staking derivatives. The goal is to give Bitcoin holders more stable borrowing conditions, deeper liquidity, and more useful collateral markets across multiple chains.

That makes Avalon less of a generic “Bitcoin DeFi” project and more of a BTC credit infrastructure play.

## BTC lending has a liquidity problem

Bitcoin is the largest collateral asset in crypto, but BTC lending is still fragmented.

Liquidity is split across chains, wrapped assets, custodians, lending venues, stablecoin systems, and isolated pools. That fragmentation creates real problems.

Borrowers face inconsistent rates and limited stablecoin liquidity.

Lenders need to understand collateral quality, liquidation paths, and counterparty assumptions.

Protocols need enough liquidity to liquidate positions without causing excessive slippage.

Avalon is trying to solve that through a mix of stable borrowing rates, multichain deployment, BTC-backed lending pools, and liquidation infrastructure designed to reduce market impact.

That is the useful part of the thesis.

BTC lending does not only need more vaults.

It needs better market structure.

## Stable rates are part of the product

Avalon’s fixed borrowing rate range, positioned around 8–10%, is one of the clearer user-facing features.

That matters because borrowers care about predictability.

Variable-rate lending can work in mature markets, but it becomes harder to manage when liquidity is thin, demand spikes quickly, or incentives distort pool behavior. For BTC holders borrowing against collateral, rate volatility can be a major UX and risk problem.

A more predictable borrowing rate gives users a clearer framework.

It does not remove risk, but it makes the position easier to reason about.

For BTCFi, that is important. Bitcoin users tend to be more cautious than typical DeFi users. If the lending experience feels unstable or opaque, they simply will not use it.

## Liquidation infrastructure is not a side detail

Avalon also emphasizes liquidation efficiency.

That may sound like backend plumbing, but in lending markets it is one of the most important parts of the system.

When collateral prices move fast, liquidations decide whether lenders stay protected and whether borrowers get wiped out cleanly or through unnecessary slippage.

Avalon’s use of proprietary high-frequency trading algorithms is meant to reduce market impact during collateral liquidations.

This is where the CeDeFi part becomes visible.

Pure on-chain liquidation can be transparent, but not always efficient. Centralized liquidity venues can offer depth, but introduce counterparty and operational assumptions. Avalon is trying to combine both sides: DeFi access and CeFi-style execution support.

That model can be useful.

It also needs clear risk disclosure.

If liquidation depends on centralized venues or off-chain execution paths, users should understand that before they deposit collateral or borrow stablecoins.

## USDa is the liquidity rail

Avalon’s USDa stablecoin is another important part of the platform.

BTC lending needs a stable unit of account. Borrowers usually do not want to borrow volatile assets against BTC. They want stable liquidity they can use elsewhere.

USDa gives Avalon a native stablecoin rail for borrowing, liquidity, and cross-chain use.

That can improve capital efficiency because the lending product is not fully dependent on external stablecoin liquidity. It also gives Avalon more control over the user flow: BTC collateral comes in, stablecoin liquidity comes out, and the broader ecosystem can build around that asset.

The key question is whether USDa becomes liquid enough to matter outside Avalon’s own controlled environments.

A stablecoin is only useful if users can move it, swap it, borrow it, repay it, and trust its liquidity under stress.

## Multichain deployment expands access

Avalon is deployed across multiple networks, including Ethereum and BNB Chain, with broader integrations across ecosystems such as Merlin Chain, Core DAO, Arbitrum, Babylon, Botanix, Kaia, and other Bitcoin-adjacent or EVM environments.

That multichain approach makes sense for BTCFi.

Bitcoin liquidity is not sitting in one place. It is spread across wrappers, L2s, staking assets, custodians, and DeFi venues. A BTC lending platform that wants real scale has to meet liquidity where it already exists.

Avalon’s multichain strategy gives it a wider surface for users and integrations.

But multichain expansion also adds complexity.

Every new chain introduces more contract risk, bridge exposure, liquidity fragmentation, oracle dependencies, and operational overhead. The upside is distribution. The risk is that the product becomes harder to understand.

The best version of Avalon is not just “available everywhere.”

It is available across the right networks with clear collateral labels, risk parameters, and exit paths.

## BTC liquid staking derivatives are a major surface

Avalon also supports BTC liquid staking derivatives.

That is important because BTCFi is moving beyond simple wrapped BTC.

Assets like staked BTC derivatives, Babylon-linked BTC assets, and other BTC yield representations are becoming part of the market. These assets need lending markets, liquidity venues, and collateral frameworks.

Avalon is building for that shift.

If BTC LSDs become a major category, lending platforms that support them early can become key liquidity venues.

But this also increases risk complexity.

A BTC LSD is not the same as native BTC. It can carry staking risk, bridge risk, issuer risk, liquidity risk, and withdrawal risk. If that asset is used as collateral, the lending market inherits all of those assumptions.

Avalon’s opportunity is to create useful credit markets around BTC derivatives.

Its responsibility is to make the risk visible.

## AVAF and the incentive layer

AVAF is Avalon’s governance token and incentive asset.

It is used to support ecosystem participation, staking, and governance. The allocation includes community incentives, team allocation, ecosystem and treasury reserves, seed and public rounds, and advisor distribution.

Token incentives can help bootstrap lending markets.

They can attract liquidity, reward early users, and support integrations.

But lending protocols are judged over time by more than emissions. The stronger signal is whether borrow demand, stablecoin liquidity, and collateral usage remain after incentives normalize.

For Avalon, AVAF should support the lending economy, not become the whole reason users show up.

## Funding and ecosystem support

Avalon Labs secured $1.5 million in seed funding in March 2024 at a $30 million valuation.

Investors include Spark Digital Capital, SNZ Holding, Matrixport, Web3Port, and Summer Ventures. Avalon was also recognized as a winner of BNB Incubation Alliance Round 2.

That kind of backing matters because BTC lending is a liquidity and partnership game.

A lending platform needs integrations with chains, stablecoin systems, custodians, market makers, liquidation venues, wallets, and ecosystem partners. Avalon’s network of partnerships across BNB Chain, Ethereum, Merlin, Core DAO, Arbitrum, Ethena, Babylon, Botanix, Kaia, and others gives it a stronger base for distribution.

The open question is how much of that translates into real borrowing and lending activity.

## Strengths of Avalon Labs

Avalon’s biggest strength is focus.

It is not trying to be every BTCFi product at once. Its core surface is BTC lending, BTC LSDs, stable borrowing, and liquidity infrastructure.

The second strength is predictable borrowing.

Fixed rates in the 8–10% range give users a clearer lending experience than fully variable markets.

The third strength is USDa.

A native stablecoin rail can improve liquidity and make the borrowing loop more complete.

The fourth strength is multichain access.

Avalon is meeting BTC liquidity across multiple environments instead of betting on one chain winning immediately.

The fifth strength is liquidation design.

Efficient liquidations matter because they protect lenders and make borrowing markets more stable during volatility.

## Weaknesses and open questions

Avalon’s biggest risk is the CeDeFi trust surface.

If centralized venues or execution systems are part of the liquidation and liquidity process, users need to understand where counterparty risk enters the system.

The second risk is BTC price dependency.

BTC-backed lending markets are highly sensitive to collateral volatility. A sharp Bitcoin drawdown can stress liquidation systems, stablecoin liquidity, and borrower behavior.

The third risk is niche concentration.

Avalon is heavily focused on BTC and BTC LSDs. That gives it clarity, but also means adoption depends on BTCFi becoming a large and durable category.

The fourth risk is multichain complexity.

More networks create more access, but also more bridge risk, contract risk, liquidity fragmentation, and integration overhead.

The fifth risk is stablecoin depth.

USDa needs real liquidity and reliable redemption or market support. Without that, the lending loop becomes less useful.

## Where Avalon fits in BTCFi

Avalon fits into the BTCFi stack as a BTC lending and liquidity layer.

Not every Bitcoin holder wants to trade.

Not every user wants to bridge into a new app chain.

But many BTC holders do want one thing: access to liquidity without selling Bitcoin.

That is where lending matters.

If Avalon can provide stable borrowing, efficient liquidations, multichain access, and usable stablecoin liquidity, it becomes part of the infrastructure that makes BTC more productive.

The market does not need more vague BTC yield narratives.

It needs products where users can see the collateral, understand the rate, price the liquidation risk, and know how they exit.

Avalon is aiming at that exact problem.

The opportunity is clear.

The hard part is proving that the platform can keep liquidity deep, rates stable, liquidations efficient, and risk disclosures clear as BTCFi moves from early users into larger capital.
