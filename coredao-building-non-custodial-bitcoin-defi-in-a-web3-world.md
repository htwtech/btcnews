CoreDAO recently published a video interview — we watched it and figured it’s worth recapping here. The project sits somewhere in the Bitcoin x DeFi space, and this conversation gave a clearer view of how they think about that.

## The Non-Custodial Foundation

As far as we understand, you don’t give them your BTC. You just sign a transaction that gets routed through a Bitcoin-secured layer. No lockups, no bridging, no wrapped assets. Funds stay in your wallet.

“You’re not giving us your Bitcoin. You’re letting it breathe on-chain, while it still belongs to you.”

The idea is to keep things simple, on-chain, and trustless.

## Scaling for Institutions

Staking originally started at 10K CORE. Now the validator requirement is 500K. According to them (and as far as we understand), this isn’t about needing more stake — it’s about showing structure that institutions are used to.

“We’re not just playing the game — we’re building a league for institutions to play in.”

How that plays out remains to be seen.

## BTC security, EVM speed

The project combines Bitcoin and Ethereum-style components. Transactions run through Bitcoin hashes, but the apps use familiar EVM tooling. That’s the pitch, at least from how they describe it.

“We route through Bitcoin hashes, but build like it’s Ethereum — scalable, fast, dev-friendly.”

Staking, swapping, and earning yield happen without leaving the ecosystem.

## Why they’re undoxxed

The team behind CoreDAO remains pseudonymous. They argue (and we tend to agree) that anonymity helps filter out distractions and that track records matter more than names.

“Being undoxxed is a feature, not a flaw. It filters for builders, not personalities.”

“People don’t stick around for a name — they stay because the work speaks for itself.”

## NFTs as an onboarding tool

They’ve also, as far as we can tell, launched a collection called Ctoshes. These NFTs aren’t about art — they’re intended as access tokens into Core-based DApps.

“If JPEGs are what get people in, we’ll make them functional too.”

A way to pull users in without starting with a whitepaper.

## Gamers at the helm

Both co-founders come from MMO culture — and reference it often. They’re building a dashboard to simplify typical DeFi actions (bridging, staking, etc.) into a single interface.

“We’ve spent decades grinding MMOs — now we grind liquidity.”

It’s not just an analogy — at least from how they talk about it, it shapes how they build.

## It’s a relationship game

One repeated theme: crypto might be built on-chain, but it runs on relationships.

“Crypto works because of relationships. We make money because of the people we trust.”

How that aligns with decentralization is a question, but it’s clearly part of how they operate.

That’s it. A quick look at what CoreDAO’s building and how they talk about it. If you want to hear it in their own words, [the full video’s on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q7N2-twPVT8).
