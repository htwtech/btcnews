# What April’s BTC Recovery Will Need To Prove In May

What April really brought back to the table was the bid. 

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF flows cranked heavily to the upside through the back half of the month, shoving institutional demand right back into the center of the move. 

![Spot Bitcoin ETF Flows](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHEmImjbsAAY4KY?format=jpg&name=small)

But what definitely did not come back nearly as fast was actual network breadth.

## Lagging Base-Layer Breadth

Active addresses are still dragging along the lower end of the past year’s range. That suggests broad base-layer participation simply hasn't recovered at the same pace as the institutional flows. The big money is moving, but the wider market isn't showing up on-chain to confirm it.

![Bitcoin Active Addresses](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHEntmuXYAA8qCQ?format=jpg&name=small)

The market did manage to repair itself in one critical way: the share of BTC supply sitting in profit rebounded hard off the March lows. This means the heavy stress regime has finally eased, and a massive chunk of holders have moved back above their cost basis. 

![BTC Supply in Profit](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHEoJ3YXAAASbzh?format=jpg&name=small)

## Structural Repair, Not a Full Reset

Looking at the tape, this still reads much more like structural repair than a full-blown demand reset. 

That is probably the cleanest, most honest way to read April. The bid came back, and the market’s internal condition stabilized. What still hasn't fully returned is the broader participation underneath the price action.

May now matters for an entirely different reason. If ETF demand stays firm and on-chain activity actually starts broadening with it, April starts to look like the foundation of something much more durable. If that on-chain breadth fails to materialize, this entire month will just look like a fragile, flow-led bounce rather than a fully confirmed expansion in demand.
