# Merlin Chain: Expanding Bitcoin Into DeFi, Gaming, and EVM Apps

Merlin Chain is one of the Bitcoin Layer 2 projects trying to push BTC beyond passive holding.

Launched in January 2024 by Bitmap Technology, Merlin Chain is built around a simple idea: Bitcoin has the strongest asset base in crypto, but its application layer is still underdeveloped.

Bitcoin is secure, liquid, and trusted, but it was not designed for fast DeFi execution, gaming, NFTs, or high-frequency application activity. Users who want those experiences usually have to move BTC into other ecosystems through wrappers, bridges, custodians, or sidechains.

Merlin is trying to make that path more direct.

The project combines Bitcoin-native assets, EVM compatibility, ZK infrastructure, decentralized oracle design, and data availability optimization to create a broader application environment around Bitcoin.

The goal is not to replace Bitcoin.

The goal is to make Bitcoin capital more usable.

## The Bitcoin utility problem

Bitcoin is still mostly treated as a store of value.

That is not a weakness by itself. It is one of the reasons Bitcoin matters. But it also creates a gap between the amount of capital sitting in BTC and the amount of activity that capital can support directly.

DeFi, NFTs, gaming, and other application categories grew much faster in ecosystems where smart contracts, fast execution, and composability were available from the start.

Bitcoin did not have that same application surface.

Merlin Chain is built for that gap.

It gives users and developers a place where Bitcoin-linked assets can move through faster, cheaper, and more flexible applications while staying connected to the broader Bitcoin ecosystem.

## What Merlin Chain does

Merlin Chain is an EVM-compatible Bitcoin Layer 2.

That means developers can build or migrate Ethereum-style applications while still targeting Bitcoin users and Bitcoin-native assets.

This is important because the developer path matters.

If a Bitcoin L2 asks every builder to learn a completely new environment, adoption becomes harder. EVM compatibility gives Merlin access to existing smart contract tooling, wallets, application patterns, and developer knowledge.

For users, the value proposition is more direct: lower fees, faster transactions, and access to DeFi, NFT, gaming, and asset-based applications connected to Bitcoin.

Merlin also supports multiple Bitcoin asset standards and protocols, including BRC-20, Bitmap, Stamp, and other Bitcoin-native formats. That gives it a broader surface than just BTC transfers or generic wrapped assets.

## ZK infrastructure and data availability

Merlin Chain uses a mix of ZK-Rollup technology, decentralized oracles, and data availability modules.

The purpose is to improve scalability while keeping the system tied to Bitcoin’s broader security and settlement narrative.

The project also uses a Two-Step ZKP Submission mechanism, designed to support verification and data submission more efficiently.

This matters because Bitcoin L2s are judged differently from normal alt-L1s.

It is not enough to say “fast and cheap.” Users also want to understand what is secured by Bitcoin, what is verified, where data lives, who controls critical infrastructure, and what assumptions sit behind deposits and withdrawals.

Merlin’s technical stack is trying to answer those questions with a blend of ZK proofs, oracle-based verification, and optimized data availability.

The real test is how these assumptions hold up as usage grows.

## Merlin’s ecosystem focus

Merlin is not only trying to be a DeFi chain.

The broader ecosystem includes DeFi, NFTs, gaming, Bitcoin-native assets, and community-driven projects.

That makes sense for Merlin’s origin.

Bitmap Technology came from the Bitcoin asset and Ordinals-adjacent side of the market, where user culture is not only about yield. It is also about collectibles, identity, gaming, and new asset experiments on Bitcoin.

That gives Merlin a different angle from some BTCFi projects that focus only on lending, staking, or liquidity.

Merlin is closer to a broad Bitcoin app layer.

If it works, users can move between Bitcoin-native assets, DeFi opportunities, NFT activity, and games without constantly leaving the ecosystem.

## Investors and funding

Merlin Chain has attracted backing from several major crypto investors and institutions.

Its financing round included OKX Ventures, ABCDE, Spartan Group, Hailstone Labs, Amber Group, Presto Labs, and other ecosystem participants.

That matters because Bitcoin L2s need more than technical infrastructure.

They need liquidity, integrations, exchange access, developer activity, wallet support, and distribution. Strong backers do not guarantee execution, but they can help a new network get into the right conversations faster.

## MERL token

MERL is the native token of Merlin Chain.

It is used for governance, staking, transaction fees, and liquidity collateral inside the ecosystem.

The total supply is 2.1 billion MERL.

The allocation is designed around ecosystem incentives, community rewards, private investors, team allocation, and public sale distribution. The largest share is directed toward ecosystem growth, which fits Merlin’s current stage: the network needs applications, users, liquidity, and recurring activity.

MERL holders can participate in staking and governance, giving the token a role in network security and ecosystem direction.

As with most early network tokens, the important question is not only token utility on paper.

It is whether token incentives create real, sticky usage or mainly short-term farming.

## Mainnet and growth

Merlin Chain launched its mainnet in early 2024.

The network gained attention quickly through its early TVL growth and the Merlin’s Seal staking campaign. Within the first weeks after launch, reported TVL crossed the multi-billion-dollar range, helped by strong early user demand and incentive activity.

The roadmap included testnet and mainnet launch in Q1 2024, asset migration tools and financing milestones in Q2, and later phases focused on MERL claiming, ecosystem growth, interoperability, and new DeFi partnerships.

That early growth showed strong market interest.

But for Merlin, the harder question is what happens after incentives and launch momentum normalize.

A Bitcoin L2 becomes important only if users keep returning for real activity: swaps, lending, liquidity, asset issuance, games, and other applications that make the chain more than a campaign destination.

## Strengths of Merlin Chain

Merlin’s biggest strength is its broad Bitcoin asset focus.

It does not limit itself to BTC alone. It also supports BRC-20, Bitmap, Stamp, and other Bitcoin-native assets, which gives the chain a larger cultural and application surface.

The second strength is EVM compatibility.

That makes it easier for developers to deploy applications and for users to interact with familiar DeFi patterns.

The third strength is early ecosystem traction.

Merlin entered the market at a moment when Bitcoin L2 attention was accelerating, and it quickly became one of the more visible projects in the category.

The fourth strength is its infrastructure stack.

By using ZK-related components, decentralized oracles, and data availability optimization, Merlin is trying to build a more scalable Bitcoin application environment rather than a simple wrapped-BTC app chain.

## Weaknesses and open questions

Merlin is still young.

That is the main risk.

Some parts of the system and ecosystem still need more real-world testing, especially under higher usage, changing market conditions, and larger capital flows.

The second risk is trust assumptions.

Like every Bitcoin L2, Merlin needs users to understand how assets move, what is verified, what is bridged, and where security assumptions sit. “Built on Bitcoin” is not enough by itself.

The third risk is incentive durability.

Early TVL can be impressive, but the stronger signal is retained activity after campaigns slow down. If usage depends mostly on rewards, the ecosystem can look larger than it really is.

The fourth risk is competition.

The Bitcoin L2 market is now crowded. Merlin competes with Stacks, Rootstock, BOB, Botanix, Bitlayer, Citrea, Mezo, Midl, and other projects trying to capture Bitcoin liquidity and developer attention.

Merlin needs to prove that its broad app-layer approach creates durable network effects.

## Where Merlin fits in BTCFi

Merlin Chain is part of the wider shift from Bitcoin as passive capital to Bitcoin as usable capital.

The project’s strongest angle is not just “DeFi on Bitcoin.” Many teams are saying that now.

Merlin’s more specific angle is that Bitcoin-native assets need a scalable application environment where users can trade, lend, play, collect, and interact without being pushed into a completely foreign ecosystem.

That is a real market need.

Bitcoin has attention.

Bitcoin has liquidity.

Bitcoin has asset culture.

What it still needs is a more usable application layer.

Merlin is one of the projects trying to build that layer with an EVM-compatible approach and support for multiple Bitcoin-native asset standards.

The opportunity is clear.

The test is whether Merlin can turn early momentum into repeated, risk-aware, real usage.

If it can, Merlin becomes more than another Bitcoin L2 narrative.

It becomes one of the places where Bitcoin assets actually start to move.
